And then you start using it. You tell Sam his number. You think you're helping. "Fifteen thousand, two hundred and four days," you say. "That's forty-one years. Use them." He laughs. Then he stops. He stops applying to college. "Then why bother?" he says. You try to take it back. You can't. The number is in his head now. Sam stops texting back. You see him in the hallway. His number is the same. He is not. You avoid your sister. Fourteen is too bright to look at. You take the long way to class. You eat lunch in the library. Three years of silence becomes hiding. You start sorting people. You sit next to the big numbers. You walk past the small ones. You catch yourself doing it. And you feel sick. When you can see everyone's ending, you stop seeing the people. You just see the countdown. You know everyone's ending. And you've never felt more alone. At night, you see the numbers through the ceiling. Through the walls. Through the dark.
