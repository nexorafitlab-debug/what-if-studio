So you start testing everyone. Your best friend, Sam. Orange hoodie. Always has your back. You check Sam. 15204. He saves you the last slice of pizza every Friday. "What?" "Nothing." Mr. Ortiz, the janitor. You check him. 9412. He waters the dead plant by the gym door every morning. "Morning." "It'll come back." Maya, the quiet girl in the library. You check her. 17842. She writes a novel in a notebook she never lets anyone read. "It's not finished." You see the mother again. On the same subway. Her number says 41. Yesterday it said 42. The baby still says 19801. The numbers are real. They move. You start doing small kindnesses. For the small numbers. You carry Mr. Ortiz's boxes. You hold doors. You give up seats. It feels good. Like you finally know what to do with it. You check the whole cafeteria. Hundreds of numbers. All at once. Most are big. Thousands of days. Years and years. A few are small. Too small. You look away. And then you see the pattern. The numbers are brightest over the people you already know. The strangers are dim. Like a light turned low. The people you love burn bright. You feel like you have a superpower. You can finally see who matters. You just don't know what "matters" costs yet.
